I was wrong, and I know it now.

a letter for you

First — the truth of it

I hurt you, and I don't want to look away from that.

There's a version of me that wants to explain, to soften the edges of what happened until it's easier to hold. But you don't need my explanations right now. You need to know that I see it plainly: I let you down, and the weight of that is mine to carry, not yours.

I keep replaying the moment I could have chosen differently and didn't. I'm not writing this to be forgiven. I'm writing it because you deserve to hear, in plain words, that I know exactly what I did.

Then — what I should have done

You trusted me with something that mattered, and I was careless with it.

Trust between people isn't a given — it's built out of small moments of showing up. I forgot that for a while. I let distraction, or pride, or my own tangled feelings get in the way of treating what we have with the care it deserves.

You are not too much. Your hurt is not an overreaction. I will not ask you to make this smaller than it is.

I don't want to be someone who apologizes and then forgets. I want this to actually change something in how I show up tomorrow, and the day after that.

And now — what I'm doing about it

Being sorry isn't a feeling I get to have once and move on from.

It's easy to say the words. It's harder to actually change the behavior that made them necessary. So this isn't just an apology for one moment — it's a promise to pay closer attention, to not let the same carelessness repeat itself, and to show that through what I do next, not just what I say now.

I'm not asking you to explain it to me again, or to manage my feelings about having hurt you. That's not your job. All I'm asking is for the chance to prove, over time, that this apology means something.

I'm not asking you to forget what happened. I'm asking for the chance to be better than the moment I let you down.

 

I'm sorry — fully, without conditions, for what I did.
